Privacy Policy

Effective [ date ] · Last reviewed 12 September 2026

LawGovern is a compliance tool for Company Secretaries and their teams. This policy explains what happens to information you put into it, who else can see that information, and what you can ask us to do about it.

It is written to be checked rather than believed. Where it describes what the software does, that behaviour is in the product and you can verify most of it yourself from the screens named.

1. Who is responsible

LawGovern is operated by [ legal name of the operating person or firm ], of [ registered address ]. In this policy “we” means that operator and “you” means the person holding a LawGovern account.

2. Two kinds of information, and two different roles

This distinction governs everything below, so it comes first.

Your own information — we are the Data Fiduciary

Your email address, your display name, your role in your practice, and the record of what you did in the product. We decide why this is held, so the responsibility under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is ours.

Your clients’ information — you are the Data Fiduciary, we are a Data Processor

Company records, directors and their DINs, meetings, charges, allotments, beneficial interests, designated persons and their trading declarations, filing references, and any document you upload.

You decide what goes in and why. We hold it and process it on your instructions and for no other purpose. We do not use it to train anything, we do not sell it, and we do not look at it except where you ask us to help with a fault you have reported.

Some of the people in those records never signed up for anything — a director whose DIN you record, an employee named as a designated person. Their information reaches us because you put it there, and your own obligations to them under the DPDP Act are not transferred to us by your using this product.

4. Where it is stored

In a Supabase project hosted in the Mumbai (ap-south-1) region, so the database sits in India. Access is enforced at the database itself by row-level security: a query from your account can only return rows belonging to a practice you are a member of. That is not a filter applied by the screen — the database refuses the rest.

Documents you upload are kept in a private storage area that serves nothing publicly. When you open one, the product mints a link that expires in five minutes. There is no public URL for any document you upload.

6. The AI features — read this one

When you use an AI feature, the text you typed leaves India. It goes to our own server function, which forwards it to OpenRouter, a company outside India, which routes it to a language-model provider. We hold the key so that it is not in your browser; we cannot control what those providers do with the text beyond their own terms.

So do not type anything into an AI feature that you would not be willing to send to a third party outside India. A client’s name, an unannounced transaction, or unpublished price-sensitive information should not go into one.

Which screens use AI, and which do not

This matters, because most of the product does not.

  • Uses AI when you ask it to: AI Compliance (the chat), Deep Law Interpretation, Resolution Drafting, Notices & Minutes drafting, and the optional summary on Regulatory Radar.
  • Never uses AI: the register, the calculators, the checklists, every entity register, the Event Impact engine, the board-minutes analysis, the applicability engine, the exceptions list, the board report and the back-test. These run entirely on your own device and our own database.

Board minutes you paste are analysed in your browser and are not sent anywhere. The Event Impact screen splits the minutes and matches each decision against the obligation register using code running on your own machine. The text is not transmitted to us, to OpenRouter, or to anyone else. It is not saved unless you save it.

An AI feature is never triggered automatically. Nothing is sent unless you click a button that says it will.

7. What is kept in your browser

The product stores a small number of viewing preferences in your browser’s local storage: which navigation groups you have collapsed, which financial year you have selected, where you had read up to in the change digest, which dashboard cards you have hidden, and your ticks on a checklist. These never leave your device and contain no client data. Clearing your browser data removes them.

8. How long we keep things

  • While your account is open: everything you have entered, because a compliance record is only useful as a history.
  • If you close your account: tell us and we will delete your account and the records belonging to your practice within [ number ] days. Export your data first — the product has a one-click export under Administration → Settings and we would rather you used it than asked us for a copy afterwards.
  • Backups may retain deleted content for a short period before they cycle out.

9. Your rights

Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, in respect of your own personal data, you may:

  • ask what we hold about you and how it has been processed;
  • have it corrected, completed or updated — most of it you can edit yourself;
  • have it erased, unless we are required to keep it;
  • withdraw consent, which for most of what is here means closing your account;
  • nominate someone to exercise these rights if you die or become incapacitated;
  • complain to us first, and then to the Data Protection Board of India if we have not resolved it.

For your clients’ data we act on your instructions. If one of your clients — or a director on one of your registers — asks us directly, we will point them to you, because you are the Data Fiduciary for that record.

10. Security, and its limits

What is in place:

  • Access is enforced by the database, not by the screen. A query for another practice’s rows returns nothing.
  • Uploaded documents are in a private store with short-lived links.
  • Passwords are hashed by the authentication provider and never reach us.
  • A filing recorded by one person can be set to require confirmation by a different person, and the database enforces that the two are not the same.
  • Every status change is recorded with who made it and when.
  • You can run an access check against your own account, from Administration → Team, and see for yourself that another practice’s data is unreachable.

What is not in place, stated plainly. The hosting is currently on a free tier. That means there is no service level agreement and no point-in-time recovery. Keep your own exports. We are not offering, and you should not assume, a guaranteed backup of your compliance record.

11. If something goes wrong

If personal data we hold is breached, we will notify the Data Protection Board of India and every affected person, in the manner and within the time the DPDP Act requires.

Grievances. Write to [ grievance officer name and email ]. We will acknowledge within [ number ] working days. If you are not satisfied with the outcome you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India.
